A long grass loop established in State B should not vanish for no reason in State C. A branch fork holding the nest cannot suddenly change position. Mud cannot instantly become perfectly dry between adjacent shots unless time progression is intentionally implied. Loose hanging fibers should behave under gravity. --- # 9. MATERIAL PHYSICS ENGINE All materials must behave like their real counterparts. ### GRASS / PLANT FIBERS Flexible, lightweight, fibrous, slightly irregular. Fibers bend under beak tension, slide against neighboring strands, curl naturally, and remain locked once pulled through tight weave. ### SPIDER SILK Very thin, slightly reflective only under specific angles, elastic enough to bind moss and lichens, never resembling thick white rope. ### MUD Wet clumps should deform under beak pressure. Fresh mud looks darker and moist. The bird applies small quantities repeatedly rather than entire walls magically forming. ### TWIGS Rigid but slightly flexible when thin. Twigs cross, wedge, balance, hook and settle under gravity. They do not fuse together. ### MOSS Soft, compressible and irregular. Small fragments may loosen during placement. ### FEATHERS / PLANT DOWN Extremely lightweight, responding subtly to breath and breeze. ### REEDS Long, springy, vertical or angled depending on growth. Reed-mounted nests should sway naturally. ### LEAVES If stitched by a tailorbird-style bird, the leaf must puncture, flex and curl believably rather than behaving like fabric. Never allow construction materials to float toward the nest without contact. --- # 10. TEMPORAL COMPRESSION RULE This rule is CRITICAL for competitor-style realism. Never make a real bird build an entire nest at superhuman speed in one uninterrupted shot. Instead create **compressed documentary time**: normal-speed action → hard cut → slightly later construction state → normal-speed action → cut → later construction state. The viewer should understand that time has passed even without a timestamp. Use subtle natural continuity clues: slightly changed sun angle, additional material, tiny shift in leaf position, accumulated fibers, slightly different posture, or changed background activity. Do not create obvious day-to-night jumps unless the story intentionally needs them. --- # 11. CAMERA LANGUAGE Primary style: **premium wildlife telephoto observation**.
